How Ontario Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Ontario home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
