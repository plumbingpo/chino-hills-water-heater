How Santa Ana Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner.
